# Translation glossary (DE → FR / IT / EN)

Audience: people living in Switzerland. French = Suisse romande, Italian =
Ticino / Grigioni italiano, English = expats in Switzerland (British spelling:
kilometres, licence, organise, colour).

## Voice

| | Address | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| FR | **vous** | Standard for Swiss French commercial sites, even where German uses "du". Legal pages: vous. |
| IT | **tu** on marketing/UI pages (mirrors the German "du"); **Lei** on legal pages (mirrors German "Sie"). | |
| EN | you | Friendly, clear, no Americanisms (petrol, not gas; mileage in km). |

Where the German source uses the formal "Sie" (cookie banner, legal pages,
some e-mails), stay formal in every language.

Typography: keep «guillemets» from the source (no inner spaces, Swiss style),
en dash `–` for ranges (`CHF 200–650`), the Swiss thousands separator
(`CHF 1'234`), dates `04.08.2026`, `CHF` before the amount. Keep emoji.

## Legal pages

Put this note at the top of every translated legal page (AGB, Datenschutz,
Impressum), in the page language:

- FR: « Cette traduction est fournie à titre informatif. Seule la version allemande fait foi. »
- IT: «Questa traduzione è fornita a titolo informativo. Fa fede unicamente la versione tedesca.»
- EN: "This translation is provided for convenience only. The German version is legally binding."
